## Player Retention

Player retention is a game's ability to keep the player engaged and playing after purchasing or downloading the game. Retention is more vital for games dependent on long-term engagement, such as live-service games, multiplayer games, and free-to-play titles. For these games, having a large initial audience does not mean it successful, as for long-term success keeping their numbers consistent is better than having a high peak and a sharp drop off in players. 
Retention is not as important for single player games, as they do not need to keep the player long-term and do not need to spend more money on constant online servers and updates. 

## The Importance of Retention

By keeping the player engaged in the game's ecosystem for longer periods of time, there are more opportunities for the game to generate more revenue. Players that log in consistently are more likely to interact with expansions, battle passes, cosmetic items, downloadable content, or other optional content that the game may offer. 

##### Factors That Influence Player Retention:

- Regular updates in content
- Multiplayer communities
- Consistent technical stability
- Engaging gameplay systems
- Consistent communication between players and developers
- Challenges and events

## Community and Player Retention

Having a strong player community can help a game keep players and consumer interest for a long-term period of time. Players creating online content, discussing the game, and introducing their friends to the game helps keep the product alive and always generating potential for new players. 

Player retention is a strong contributor to [[sales-performance|Sales Performance]], as keeping players engaging with the game provides more opportunities to generate more revenue. However, balancing consistent updates with the player expectations can be a difficult challenge. Making meaningful updates to a game takes time, and if the updates take too long the player might move on to something new and not come back. Yet, if the updates are very frequent, but lack much depth in new content, the player will lose interest and move on as well. 

Many successful games have mastered the ability to bring players back even after they leave for extended periods of time, creating long-term sustainability and revenue. This is the reason that player retention is a valuable and important factor to be considered when evaluating video game [[commercial-success-and-failure|Commercial Success and Failure]].
