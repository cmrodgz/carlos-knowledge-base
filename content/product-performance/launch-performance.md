## Launch Performance

A game's launch is the time period right after the initial release to consumers. This period is very important because it can set the precedent for how the game will perform long-term and is how a large portion of the outside public will see the game for the first time. Having a strong launch can boost momentum that can carry the game throughout it's entire life cycle, while a weak launch can make it for a game to never be able to recover or become profitable. 

## How to Measure a Launch

Launch performance can be measured with several indicators. Initial sales in the first days and weeks, along with the player number counts, show how much audience attention the game is receiving. Initial online discussions and content show how players are responding to the game and how they feel about the product. 

## The Significance of First Impressions

The game's reputation can be majorly impacted by the first impressions it gives the player. A game releasing with technical issues can leave negative lasting impressions and result in players even refunding the game. 

This is where [[critical-reception|Critical Reception]] is vital, as reviews and player discussions influence the public's perception of the game and either boosts or hurts [[sales-performance|Sales Performance]]. A strong launch results in building an audience that will stay with the game long-term, helping the game's [[player-retention|Player Retention]].

However, first impressions are not always the deciding factor of a game's success. Games have had bad launches, yet, through updates and fixes after release, games have been able to recover. Therefore, launch performance is important to analyze when discussing video game success, but it should be measured as a contributing factor to the overall success, rather than the main cause.
