# Sales Performance

Sales performance is one of the best available methods to measure a video games success in the consumer market. Copies sold, downloads, and digital purchases all provide insight onto how consumers view a product and how likely they are to spend money to support it. However, sales numbers do not always show the complete picture for a game, as games with higher marketing and development costs require much higher sales to be profitable. 


## Factors Influencing Sales

- Brand Influence: Highly regarded game developers, studios, and franchises can boost a games sales by attracting consumers who already recognize and trust the brand. 
- Available Platforms: A game being available on more platforms increases the accessibility of the product and gets exposed to a larger audience, meaning more potential sales. 
- Price: The price of a game at launch directly influences how likely a consumer is willing to buy the game. 
- Reviews: Positive critic reviews and word of mouth from players can influence other consumers to purchase the game. 
- Marketing: Strong advertisement campaigns help increase exposure to consumers and help increase overall interest in the game. 

## Long-Term Performance and Sales

Games do not always sell at consistent levels throughout their lifetime. Some games experience a majority of their sales at launch, while other see gradual increases over time through updates, additional content, word of mouth, and discounts. This makes having a great [[launch-performance|Launch Performance]] an essential factor for a game to achieve a better sales performance. 

Sales are also directly impacted by [[critical-reception|Critical Reception]], as players can influence outside audiences to purchase the product. However, poor reception has the opposite effect, driving away potential consumers that were hesitant about making the purchase. 

In conclusion, high sales numbers are a important factor in the overall commercial performance of game, but they should be analyzed accordingly by factoring in several other variables including player retention, development and marketing costs, and long-term revenue.
