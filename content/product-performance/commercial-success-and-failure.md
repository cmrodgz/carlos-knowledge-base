## Commercial Success and Failure

The commercial success and failure of a video game is determined by several different factors. Sales are important, but other factors that should be considered are marketing expenses, long-term revenue, critical reception, player engagement, and development costs. Games can sell millions of copies, but still end up not being profitable because of outside expenses. 

> "Well, what's worked for me so far is finding something I want to make that matches up with what the market currently needs." - Shigeru Miyamoto
Source: https://shmuplations.com/success1989/ 

## Why Games Can Succeed

A successful game does not just succeed in one category. Instead, several factors work together to contribute to a game's success. A strong [[launch-performance|Launch Performance]] helps build an initial audience, while positive [[critical-reception|Critical Reception]] improves how the game is perceived by the public. A game having continued [[player-retention|Player Retention]] keeps the community alive and creates continuous opportunities for revenue. 

#### Characteristics of Successful Games

- Strong marketing
- Engaging gameplay
- Continuous support and updates
- A stable business model
- Reliable technical performance
- A specific target audience
- Good word of mouth 

## Why Games Can Fail

A game can fail for several reasons. A game being released into a overly competitive market, experiencing development issues, having technical issues on launch, or missing its intended audience can all lead to a game failing. Having a poor [[sales-performance|Sales Performance]] leads to a higher difficulty to make a return on investment for the development studio and publisher of the game. 

There is a strong connection between the factors that work together to cause a game to fail. A poor launch results in negative reviews, causing a reduction in sales. Having lower sales means there are less resources to support the game after launch, hurting player retention. This demonstrates the relationship between the different aspects of [[product-performance/index|Product Performance]] and how they influence each other. 

In conclusion, there is no guaranteed way to predict the success of a game. The video game market is constantly changing and is very competitive. The preferences of consumers change over time and high expectations can end up hurting a game more than helping it. By taking into account all of these connected performance factors, we can create a better picture as to why certain games succeed while others fail.
