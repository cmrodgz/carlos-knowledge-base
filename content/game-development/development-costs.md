# Overview

Creating a video game requires a large amount of financial resources. These development costs include employee salaries, hardware, software, office expenses, licensing, technology, testing, and other production needs. 

Development costs are important to take into consideration because it determines how much revenue a game needs to generate in order to be profitable and commercially successful. 

## Major Expenses

The biggest expense when developing a game is usually labor expenses to pay developers and other specialists working on creating the game. Then, these projects sometimes require specialized software and other technological resources, adding to the development cost even more. The bigger a project is, the more employees and longer production periods are required, increasing the [[development-timelines-and-delays|Development Timelines and Delays]]. 

## Factors Increasing Costs

- Advanced graphics
- Larger development teams
- Longer development timeline
- Post-launch updates and support
- Online features and multiplayer
- Larger game worlds
- More extensive testing
- More content on launch

## Financial Risk

A game with higher development costs result in a project with increased financial risk. This is because it would require much stronger sales to recover the initial investment. This factor is even more important when the game is entering a market that is highly saturated or competitive, as discussed in [[market-saturation-and-competition|Market Saturation and Competition]].

## Development Costs and Commercial Performance

Games with high development costs does not guarantee a game will be successful. Similarly, a low-budget game can end up making a large return on investment. 

What development costs do end up affecting is the financial expectations for a game and how much it needs to make in order to be regarded as a commercial success. This is something we look at when reviewing a game's [[sales-performance|Sales Performance]] and [[commercial-success-and-failure|Commercial Success and Failure]].
