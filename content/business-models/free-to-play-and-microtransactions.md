# Overview

Free-to-play business models allow gamers to access the game without paying an upfront purchase price. Instead, the game generates revenue from microtransactions and other optional purchases from within the free game. 

This model has become a big part of the modern video game market because it allows more consumers to engage with video games at little or no cost. This increases the potential audience and creates more opportunity to generate revenue over time.

## How It Works

This model offers several different types of purchases to the player. This includes cosmetic items, DLC content, battle passes, and other optional game features. Since the barrier for entry is free, games can reach much larger player numbers and have more opportunities to gain revenue from them. This model directly affects [[player-purchasing-decisions|Player Purchasing Decisions]].

## Consumer Advantages

The free-to-play model eliminates the initial financial barrier to play a game. Consumers can engage with the game thoroughly before deciding if they want to spend money on it. This is appealing for players who are cautious about purchasing games at premium or full prices. 

## Free-To-Play and Commercial Performance

Video game companies have to decide if a free-to-play business model is the best choice for their project goals. This business model creates recurring revenue opportunities over time, instead of relying so heavily on initial sales purchases and discounts like [[premium-priced-games|Premium Priced Games]] do. 

However, a free-to-play business model needs to maintain enough active players and engagement from consumers to keep purchasing items in the game. There is also a lot of competition in this market, with games such as Fortnite or Overwatch taking many free-to-play players from other games. 

This business model relies heavily on [[player-retention|Player Retention]] numbers when evaluating if a free-to-play game is considered a long-term [[commercial-success-and-failure|Commercial Success or Failure]].
