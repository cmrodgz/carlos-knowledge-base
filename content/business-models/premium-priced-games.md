# Overview

This business model relies on pricing their games at a premium and full-price to generate revenue from upfront initial payments. This model is common for major console and PC releases, requiring the consumer to pay a premium price before having access to the full product. 

The pricing of a game influences a consumer's purchasing decision-making and how much revenue a company needs to make to become profitable. 

## Game Development and Pricing

The pricing of a premium game is directly connected to the amount of marketing and development expenses required to make the game. Video game companies must consider how much the game needs to make in revenue to recover the costs of these expenses. Therefore, when deciding if a game should be sold at a premium price, [[development-costs|Development Costs]] and [[video-game-advertising|Video Game Advertising]] expenses must be considered. 

## Market Competition and Consumer Options

A game at a premium price can make players feel more selective about making a purchase, since the player usually has various alternatives where they can spend their money in the entertainment industry. The rest of the market can result in a premium priced game being perceived as overpriced and hurt its revenue. This is why [[market-saturation-and-competition|Market Saturation and Competition]] should be considered when deciding whether or not a game is worth being priced at a premium. 

## Premium Priced Games and Commercial Performance

Sales performance is a very important measure for premium priced games, since they are very reliant on game purchases to make revenue. A game may have to receive strong initial sales or continue getting sales over time to recover investment costs and generate revenue.

The game experiencing discounts and eventual price reductions can bring in more revenue long after the games initial release. This is why it is important to consider [[launch-performance|Launch Performance]] and [[sales-performance|Sales Performance]]of a premium priced game when evaluating whether it is commercially successful.
